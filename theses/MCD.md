## Why I'm looking at it
McDonald's is the world's largest restaurant brand. Most of its restaurants are run by franchisees, who pay McDonald's rent and royalties, so the company earns from property and fees more than from selling burgers. It has raised its dividend for **50 years in a row** (to $1.93 a quarter, announced 17 Sep 2026), and at $235.56 (9 Oct) it trades at ~17× forward earnings, the lowest in over a decade, with a ~3.3% yield.

It also has a crash record: in 2008 it was one of only two Dow stocks to finish the year up, because people switched to cheaper food.

## Why it's cheap (the honest part)
Uncorrelated doesn't mean safe. MCD fell **~32% from its high** over the same year the Nasdaq rose. That fall came from its own business, not the market:
- **US sales are going backwards.** US comparable sales were +0.8% in Q2, driven by price while customer visits fell. July and August were negative, and management expects Q3 to be **slightly negative**.
- **A self-inflicted value mistake.** Cutting digital offers to fund the under-$3 menu was, in the CEO's words, a "bad trade". Only 60–65% of restaurants followed the recommended pricing. The head of McDonald's USA was replaced in August.
- **Costs.** US beef rose ~14% last year and has nearly doubled in five years.
- **Investor Day (23 Sep 2026)** set out the "NEXT" plan: ~$8.5B of franchisee support through 2036 (~$5B by 2030) for remodels and tech, aiming for +2.5 pts of restaurant margin and an operating margin in the low-to-mid 50s% by 2030 (47% in H1 2026). The 50,000-restaurant target slipped from 2027 to 2028. The stock fell ~5% that day, its worst day since March 2020.

**The bet:** the US slump is mostly self-inflicted and fixable (value pricing and execution), not a permanent loss of customers. The franchise and property model and the dividend keep paying while it gets fixed.

**What I'm not buying:** the story that "institutions are quietly loading up on MCD before a crash". The filings show small advisers trading both ways, and holdings as of 30 Sep aren't public until mid-November. The price trend says the opposite of accumulation.

## What has to be true
- [ ] US customer visits stop falling within the next two to three quarters as the value menu settles
- [ ] The NEXT spending shows up as higher franchisee cash flow, not just higher costs for McDonald's
- [ ] The dividend keeps growing (it's management's second capital priority after reinvestment)
