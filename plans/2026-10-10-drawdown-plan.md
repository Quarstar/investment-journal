# Drawdown plan · written 10 Oct 2026, before any correction

> Written while the Nasdaq-100 (QQQ) sits about 1% below its record close. The point of committing this now is that I can't rewrite it once a fall starts.

## Why I'd buy a correction
I think the AI build-out is a multi-year cycle, like railways and the internet. A correction would let me add to it at better prices.

**The caveat I'm holding myself to:** in both of those cycles the technology won while many early investors lost. Britain's railway shares collapsed after 1845, and the Nasdaq fell about 78% from 2000 to 2002 and took until 2015 to recover. So in a drawdown I buy **survivors with real cash flow**, I buy **in stages**, and I assume the fall can last **years, not weeks**.

## Conditions right now (context, not triggers)
- US 10-year yield ~5.3%, highest since 2002. Fed hiked in Sept and signals another hike by year-end.
- OpenAI's annualised revenue reported near $50B vs ~$70B previously signalled, against very large compute commitments. That's the main risk to my book.
- AI-linked holdings are ~55% of the portfolio. A 30% fall in those names would cost the book roughly 15–20%.

## Thesis check before every buy
A name only gets money if all of these still hold at its latest earnings:
- [ ] Revenue or backlog still growing
- [ ] No large new share issuance since my last buy
- [ ] Its biggest customers haven't cut AI capex guidance
- [ ] Buying keeps it under its caps (20% single stock, 35% energy/nuclear, 15% neocloud)

If hyperscalers cut capex, the thesis is weaker and I stop deploying until I've reassessed. Prices falling for that reason aren't a discount.

## Fallback: the Nasdaq-100 index (added 11 Oct 2026)
When I'm unsure which names will survive, the index is the default instead of guessing.

**What I'd buy:** a UK-listed Nasdaq-100 UCITS ETF, such as Invesco EQQQ (ticker EQQQ, London). QQQ itself is US-listed and usually can't be bought in a UK ISA. I'll check which version my ISAs offer before a fall, not during one.

**When a tranche's money goes to the index instead of a single name:**
- A buy-list name fails the thesis check above, and no other name on the list has room under its caps.
- I can't tell which of the core names the market is punishing for a real reason. In that case up to **half** of the tranche goes to the index.
- Single names in the book would breach the 20% cap or the energy/nuclear and neocloud caps.

**Rules that still apply:**
- The same triggers (−15%, −25%, −35%) and the same two-week spacing. The index fell ~78% in 2000–02 and took until 2015 to recover, so it isn't safe from a long fall. It just can't go to zero.
- The index is heavy in NVDA and GOOGL, so index money counts toward my AI-linked share.
- Index units bought in a drawdown are held for at least two years, not swung.

## What I won't do

- Spend the whole shield on the first drop.
- Average down on swing trades.
- Change this plan during a fall. Changes only happen at a Wednesday review, with the reason written down and committed.
