## Marvell (MRVL)

**The case:** it's the "chips talking to each other" layer: custom AI chips for the hyperscalers, plus the electro-optics and networking between them. Lumentum adds the lasers and optical parts, so the combined company would cover the copper-to-light shift end to end.

**The flag: the Lumentum deal changes what I'd be buying.** Announced 2 Nov: all-stock, 4.5 Marvell shares per Lumentum share, a $115bn enterprise value, a ~24% premium. Marvell holders end up with ~67% of the combined company, so **the share count rises by about half**. A third of what I'd own would be Lumentum, bought at a premium after it more than doubled this year. The deal has weighed on the stock since (Motley Fool, 16 Nov). Close date and approvals not verified.

**Read:** watch, don't buy. Wait for the **1 Dec print** (never into a print) and for the deal terms to settle. It is also the most AI-capex-sensitive of the three (beta 2.3), so it rotates out of the same risk NVDA carries rather than away from it.

## GE Vernova (GEV)

**The case:** the grid is the choke point after chips: transformer lead times ~100 weeks and ~44% of new projects waiting 4+ years for a grid connection (bank estimates via MarsBit, May 2026). Record backlog, turbine slots sold years ahead, net cash, a falling share count, and three guidance raises this year.

**The flags:** the richest of the three at ~34× forward. **Electrification orders fell 9% organically**, the grid business specifically. Wind losses widened to $400–500M. Most of Q3's free cash flow ($5.3bn of $5.4bn) was customer down payments, not profit. The $9bn Trench deal is half debt. Policy risk after the midterms (possible action against data-centre building).

**Read:** the best business of the three and the closest match to the next bottleneck, but it isn't cheap yet. It needs a capture-zone price before it's a candidate. Least tied to AI chips: it also wins from electrification in general.

## Besi (BESI)

**The case:** close to a monopoly in hybrid bonding, the tool that stacks chips and memory with no solder bumps, which HBM and advanced packaging need more of each generation. Bernstein expects Besi to ship about three of every four hybrid bonders in 2028. 46% EBITDA margin; orders nearly doubled year on year.

**The flag: one binary risk.** ASML appears to be developing a rival hybrid-bonding tool (SemiAnalysis report, October; ASML hasn't confirmed). That's why the stock halved and BofA downgraded it. Besi's CEO says ASML isn't a competitor there. If ASML enters seriously, the growth story shrinks. If it doesn't, the stock is priced for a threat that didn't come. Q4 guided flat (customers moved deliveries into H1 2027 production plans).

**Read:** the most asymmetric of the three and the most "not fully realised", which is also why it should start **sandbox-sized** (≤5% sandbox; ~2 points of room today). Priced in euros, so it adds a currency exposure. Check Trading 212 lists it in the ISA.
